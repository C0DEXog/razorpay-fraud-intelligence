CHAPTER 1: THE BIG STORY — THE MAGIC ICE CREAM SHOP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Imagine you run a super popular ice cream shop in school.

Hundreds of kids buy ice cream every day.
- Most kids are good: they hand you Rs 20, take their chocolate cone, and walk away happy.
- But 1 or 2 sneaky kids use FAKE MONOPOLY MONEY, or they steal someone else's lunch card to buy 50 ice creams in 5 minutes!

If you check every single coin by hand with a magnifying glass, the line gets too long and good kids leave angry.
If you don't check at all, you lose all your ice cream and get left with fake paper money.

OUR PROJECT IS A SUPER SMART ROBOT SECURITY GUARD.
Every time someone orders an ice cream, the robot looks at the clues in less than a blink of an eye (20 milliseconds) and says:
- "0.05 Risk — This is little Timmy buying his daily vanilla cone. Let him pass!"
- "0.95 Risk — Someone is ordering 100 mango tubs at 3:00 AM from a brand new phone with a fake card. HOLD UP! Check their ID!"

CHAPTER 2: HOW WE GET DATA (APIs, ORDERS, AND RECEIPTS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Before our robot can catch bad guys, it needs to see what's happening.

1. Razorpay:
   The digital cash register used by shops like Swiggy, Zomato, and Cred. It connects the customer's bank to the shop's bank.

2. API (Application Programming Interface):
   A magical tube or waiter that delivers messages between computers. We ask the Razorpay waiter: "Give me the last 300 orders!" and it brings them on a tray.

3. Endpoint (/v1/orders and /v1/payments):
   The specific counter at the restaurant. If you go to the /orders window, you get order receipts. If you go to /payments, you see if the cash actually landed.

4. API Key & Secret (.env file):
   Your secret password and VIP badge. Kept in a secret locker (.env) so hackers cannot steal your register access.

5. Paise vs Rupees:
   Razorpay counts money in pennies/paise (Rs 1 = 100 paise). When the API says 50000, our code divides by 100 to make it Rs 500.

6. CSV File:
   An Excel sheet with neat rows and columns. Like a notebook where every line is one transaction.

CHAPTER 3: THE 24 CLUES (FEATURE ENGINEERING EXPLAINED)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Raw data is boring: it just says "Order #123, Rs 500, Tuesday, 2 AM".
A detective needs CLUES (we call them FEATURES). We turn basic facts into 24 smart clues:

--- GROUP A: THE SPEED CLUES ("VELOCITY") ---
(If a kid walks into your shop once a day, normal. If they sprint in 10 times in 5 minutes, suspicious!)

1. cnt_1 (Count in 1 Hour):
   How many times did this buyer swipe their card in the last 60 minutes? (1 swipe = normal; 15 swipes = thief testing stolen cards!).
2. cnt_6 (Count in 6 Hours):
   Total swipes in 6 hours.
3. cnt_24 (Count in 24 Hours):
   Total swipes in a whole day.
4. cnt_24_log (Log of 24h Count):
   Squashes giant numbers so the robot doesn't get confused if a big supermarket does 10,000 orders.
5. amt_sum_1 (Money spent in 1 Hour):
   Total cash moved in 60 minutes.
6. amt_sum_6 (Money spent in 6 Hours):
   Total cash moved in 6 hours.
7. amt_sum_24 (Money spent in 24 Hours):
   Total cash moved in a full day.
8. amt_mean_24 (Average spent in 24h):
   Is their average order Rs 50 or Rs 50,000?
9. amt_ratio_6 (6-Hour Money Spike):
   Did they suddenly spend 10 times more money in the last 6 hours than their normal average?
10. burst_6 (Sudden Burst):
   A rapid explosion of purchases (like machine-gun card testing).

--- GROUP B: THE CLOCK CLUES ("TEMPORAL & CYCLICAL") ---
(Computers are dumb with clocks: they think 11:59 PM and 12:01 AM are 23 hours apart! We fix that with round math.)

11. hour_sin & 12. hour_cos (Clock Circle Math):
   We wrap the 24 hours of a day into a round clock circle using sine and cosine. Now the computer knows 11:59 PM is right next to 12:01 AM!
13. dow_sin & 14. dow_cos (Day of Week Circle):
   Wraps Sunday around to Monday smoothly.
15. is_night:
   Is it between 11 PM and 5 AM? (Honest people are sleeping; fraudsters work late at night).
16. is_weekend:
   Is it Saturday or Sunday?

--- GROUP C: THE PRICE & BEHAVIOR CLUES ---
17. amount_log:
   The size of the bill, converted to a math scale so Rs 10 and Rs 1,00,000 can be compared fairly.
18. amount_round100:
   Is the amount an exact round number like Rs 500, Rs 1000, Rs 5000? (Legit stores charge Rs 499 or Rs 1,249. Fraudsters often test flat Rs 1000s).
19. amount_round50:
   Is it a multiple of Rs 50?
20. device_age_log:
   How old is this phone or laptop? (A phone used for 2 years is trustworthy; a phone created 10 minutes ago is risky).
21. amt_dev_cat (Category Deviation):
   If you buy a Rs 5,000 chocolate, that's crazy high! If you buy a Rs 5,000 laptop, that's normal. This clue checks if the price is weird FOR THAT SPECIFIC ITEM TYPE.
22. cat_digital_goods:
   Is it gift cards or game coins? (Thieves love digital goods because they can't be shipped back).
23. cat_travel:
   Is it flight tickets?
24. cat_electronics:
   Is it phones/gadgets?

CHAPTER 4: THE ROBOT BRAINS: HOW THE AI LEARNS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Decision Tree (The Flowchart):
   Think of a game of 20 Questions.
   - "Is it night?" -> Yes.
   - "Is it a brand new phone?" -> Yes.
   - "Is the amount round?" -> Yes.
   - Conclusion: 90% chance it's fraud!

2. LightGBM (The Super-Fast Grandmaster):
   Instead of 1 flowchart, LightGBM builds hundreds of flowcharts in a chain.
   - Flowchart #1 makes a few guesses.
   - Flowchart #2 looks at where Flowchart #1 made mistakes and corrects them.
   - Flowchart #3 corrects Flowchart #2.
   It is blazing fast (takes 0.01 seconds) and runs on simple CPU chips without needing expensive supercomputers.

3. Random Forest (The Jury Room):
   Imagine asking 300 different detectives to vote independently.
   - Detective 1 looks at time and amount.
   - Detective 2 looks at device age and category.
   - Detective 3 looks at velocity.
   They all vote! If 270 detectives shout "FRAUD!", the risk score is 90%.

4. The Ensemble (Two Heads Are Better Than One):
   We combine LightGBM (the speed master) + Random Forest (the wise jury). We take the average of their scores. If both agree, we are 100% confident!

5. Temporal Split (No Time Travel / No Cheating!):
   If you want to test if a student is good at math, you give them NEW questions they have never seen, not yesterday's homework with answers!
   - We train our AI on Past Orders (older time window).
   - We test our AI on Future Orders (newer time window).
   This proves our AI works on brand new tomorrow's fraud, not just memorizing the past.

CHAPTER 5: THE SCORECARD: HOW WE GRADE THE AI (METRICS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In school, a test is graded from 0 to 100. But in fraud detection, regular Accuracy is a scam!

Why Accuracy is a Trap:
If 99 transactions are legit and only 1 is fraud, a dumb robot that says "EVERYTHING IS LEGIT" is 99% accurate... but it missed the thief 100% of the time!

So we use real, honest metrics:

1. PR-AUC (Precision-Recall Area Under Curve):
   - What it means: The overall "Gold Medal Score" of our detective on super hard, imbalanced data.
   - Scale: 0.0 (blind guess) to 1.0 (perfect psychic).
   - Our Score (~0.67): Excellent for real-world noisy fraud data where fraud is very rare.

2. Precision@K (Accuracy among the Top Flagged):
   - If the robot rings the alarm bell on the 30 most suspicious kids (K=30), how many of them actually had fake money?
   - If 27 out of 30 were real thieves, Precision@30 = 90%.
   - Why it matters: Security guards have limited time. We don't want them wasting hours checking innocent kids!

3. Recall@K (Catch Rate):
   - Out of all the thieves in the entire school, how many did our robot catch?
   - If there were 10 thieves and we caught 8 of them, Recall = 80%.

4. FP Rate @ K (False Alarm Rate):
   - How often did we accidentally accuse an innocent good kid?
   - Why it matters: If you accuse a good customer of stealing, they will get mad and delete the app forever. We keep this rate super low!

CHAPTER 6: THE "WHY DID YOU CATCH ME?" AUDIT: SHAP VALUES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Imagine a police officer arrests someone and the judge asks: "Why did you arrest him?"
The police officer can't just say: "Because my crystal ball said so." That's illegal!
In banking and RBI regulations, you MUST explain every decision.

Here is how SHAP works:
- Normal Baseline Risk = 0.10
- PLUS Transacted at 3 AM ................ (+0.30)
- PLUS 6 Purchases in 10 mins ............ (+0.25)
- PLUS Brand New Unverified Phone ........ (+0.20)
- MINUS Long-time Verified Customer ...... (-0.05)
-------------------------------------------------
- FINAL RISK SCORE = 0.80 (FLAGGED!)

1. SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations):
   A Nobel Prize-winning math formula from game theory. It breaks down the final score and assigns exact points (positive or negative) to each clue.

2. Base Value:
   The average risk of any random normal order in the system (e.g., 0.12).

3. SHAP Waterfall Plot:
   A step-by-step staircase picture:
   - Red stairs push the risk UP (night time, high speed, round amount).
   - Blue stairs pull the risk DOWN (day time, known device, normal price).
   - The final landing is the Exact Risk Score.

CHAPTER 7: THE CONTROL ROOM: DASHBOARD & SLIDERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When you open http://localhost:8501, you are sitting in the pilot's cockpit:

1. Sidebar Button ("Fetch Live from Razorpay API"):
   Calls real Razorpay servers, grabs the freshest 300 orders and payments, feeds them through our 24 clues, retrains the models, and updates all charts in 3 seconds!

2. Threshold Slider (0.0 to 1.0):
   Like the sensitivity dial on a smoke detector.
   - Set to 0.30 (super sensitive): Catches every tiny thief, but rings alarms on a few innocent toast burners.
   - Set to 0.85 (strict): Only rings when the house is fully on fire.

3. Review Capacity Slider (K):
   Tells the dashboard: "My team only has time to inspect 20 transactions today." The dashboard immediately gives you the Top 20 most dangerous transactions in order.

4. Merchant Risk Tab:
   Groups transactions by shop owner. If 80% of transactions from "Shop #4" are fraudulent, Razorpay can freeze that shop's payouts immediately.
